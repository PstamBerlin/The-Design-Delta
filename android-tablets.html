01 / The landscape

The landscape

A tablet is a panel, a battery, a system-on-chip and a back cover, and of those four the panel is usually the most expensive. What makes this category worth a page is not the bill of materials but how it is sold. Unlike almost everything else in this atlas, an Android tablet arrives as a turnkey solution: the silicon vendor supplies not only the SoC but the reference board, the board support package, a working Android build and the driver stack on top of it, so the house you hire is choosing an industrial design, a panel, a battery and a certification path rather than engineering a computer. Allwinner in Zhuhai describes its own business in exactly those words. This is the model the front page of this guide describes, surviving in its purest form.

It is worth understanding why tablets remained open when phones closed. The hireable layer in smartphones consolidated into three houses, Huaqin, Longcheer and Wingtech, which between them take roughly 75 percent of outsourced smartphone shipments and none of which is in the Pearl River Delta; the delta kept the brands, OPPO and vivo in Dongguan, Honor and Transsion in Shenzhen, but brands do not put your name on anything. Tablets escaped that consolidation because the barriers are lower at every step: no carrier approvals, no modem certification at all if the device is WiFi-only, a slower replacement cycle, and a very large customer base that does not want a consumer product in the first place.

The gate, when there is one, is Google. To ship a tablet with the Play Store and the Google apps you need a Mobile Application Distribution Agreement and GMS certification. Google does not charge for the licence itself, but the third-party lab testing it requires runs on the order of ten to fifteen thousand dollars per model, and that is per model, which is precisely what kills a catalogue of forty near-identical SKUs. So a great deal of the delta's tablet volume has moved to plain AOSP, where Google is simply not involved: education and kids tablets, point-of-sale terminals, warehouse handhelds, medical carts, in-vehicle and kiosk displays. In most of those the customer is installing its own application anyway and the Play Store would be a liability rather than a feature. Decide which side of that line you are on before you shortlist an ODM, because it changes the house, the bill of materials and the schedule.

Then ask the question that catches people out, which is about time rather than cost: which Android version, on which board support package, patched for how long. Turnkey platforms are frequently offered on an older BSP because it is stable and cheap, and a tablet whose security patches stopped two years ago is increasingly not a product you can sell in Europe. The delta covers this category end to end, silicon in Zhuhai, panels and assembly in Shenzhen, which means you can source the whole thing inside a two-hour drive. It also means nobody in that chain is incentivised to raise the software-support question for you.

For export the hardware gates are routine: CE and UKCA, FCC, RoHS, RED for the radio, and UN38.3 for shipping the cell. Two newer EU obligations deserve planning time. Since 28 December 2024 the Common Charger Directive requires USB-C and harmonised USB Power Delivery above 15 watts, with labelling, covered on the cables and connectors page. Larger by far is the Cyber Resilience Act, in force since 10 December 2024: from 11 September 2026 manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities to ENISA within 24 hours, and from 11 December 2027 the main obligations bite, at which point a product with digital elements cannot carry CE marking without them. For a connected Android device sold into Europe that turns the software-support question above from good practice into a condition of market access.

02 / Solution houses

The solution houses

01

Allwinner 全志科技

Zhuhai

The platform, and the reason this category still belongs to the delta. Incorporated in Zhuhai in 2007 and listed in Shenzhen as 300458 since 2015, Allwinner designs application SoCs together with the analogue parts and wireless connectivity around them, and sells them as turnkey solutions rather than as bare silicon. It was the world's largest supplier of Android tablet application processors by unit shipments in 2012 and 2013, and the same platform approach now reaches into set-top boxes, IP cameras, smart displays, automotive and the single-board computers that hobbyists know it for. Revenue reached 2.29 billion yuan in 2024, up nearly 37 percent. The house whose reference design your ODM is quietly reselling.

02

Alldocube 酷比魔方

Shenzhen

Founded 1995, which makes it older than the product it sells, Shenzhen Alldocube is the consumer end of the delta's tablet trade. The iPlay line runs Android and the iWork line runs Windows on two-in-ones, aimed squarely at the value bracket beneath Samsung and Lenovo, with its own research, production and sales rather than a pure trading operation, and a State Hi-Tech Enterprise designation behind it. It works across Intel, Microsoft, Qualcomm, MediaTek and Spreadtrum platforms, which is the useful signal here: a house fluent in several silicon families can be argued out of the one its engineers find most convenient, which a single-platform shop cannot.

03

Emdoor 亿道信息

Shenzhen

The other half of the market, and the more profitable one. Shenzhen Emdoor Information was founded in 2008 by Zane Zhang and listed on the Shenzhen exchange in February 2023, building rugged handheld terminals, rugged tablets and rugged laptops on OEM and ODM terms, with long-standing Intel and Microsoft relationships. This is where the AOSP argument above becomes concrete: a warehouse handheld or a field terminal runs the customer's own application, needs drop and ingress ratings rather than a Play Store, and sells at several times the margin of a consumer slab. If your tablet is going into a vehicle, a hospital or a loading bay, this is the class of house to shortlist.

04

Tianma 深天马

Shenzhen · Longhua

The panel, which is to say the most expensive object in the box. Tianma Microelectronics was established in November 1983, listed in Shenzhen as 000050 in March 1995, sits under the AVIC umbrella and works out of Longhua. It built China's first TN-LCD production line in 1984 and was the first in the country to reach mass production on a G5.5 LTPS line, and it now spans LTPS, AMOLED and Micro-LED across smartphones, automotive, tablets and IT displays, holding a leading global position in small and medium panel module shipments. Included because tablet buyers negotiate hard on the SoC and then accept whatever glass the ODM proposes, which is the wrong way round.
