01 / The landscape

The landscape

The category arrives in two shapes. One is a pendant or a pin, worn on a collar, that captures audio all day and hands it to a model which transcribes, summarises and later answers questions like who you met and what you agreed. The other is a pair of glasses with a camera on the temple, which does the same and can see. The bill of materials is modest either way: a microphone array, a small camera, Bluetooth and WiFi, a very small cell, and almost no local compute, because the thinking happens on somebody's server. The engineering problem is not the intelligence, it is thermal and battery in an object worn on a face for sixteen hours. Production-class camera glasses land under about 40 grams, and every gram is argued over.

Now the part that makes this page different from earbuds or action cameras: the platform is not designed here. The dominant reference design for AI glasses is Qualcomm's Snapdragon AR1, productised into a build-ready package by Thundercomm, a ThunderSoft and Qualcomm joint venture headquartered in San Diego with its Chinese engineering in Beijing and Chongqing, offered to ODMs through Qualcomm's Snapdragon START programme at under 40 grams and sixteen hours. The pendant brands are American too. Limitless was acquired by Meta and stopped selling on 5 December 2025, Bee went to Amazon, Friend and Omi are San Francisco companies, and Plaud is a Delaware corporation run out of San Francisco. What the Pearl River Delta owns in this category is not the platform. It is the two hardest physical layers, the optics and the build, plus a genuinely strong brand bench.

There is a tell, and it doubles as the most useful diligence trick on this site. Plaud's NotePin carries FCC ID 2A6T3-PN0200, and the grantee on that record is Shenzhen Smart Connect Technology; one of Plaud's co-founders is a Shenzhen factory owner. That is the shape of the whole pendant category, an American brand and a Shenzhen builder, and it hands you a free lookup. Take any AI wearable, find the FCC ID printed on the device or buried in its manual, search the FCC grantee database, and you will usually be looking at the name of the company that actually made it. It works on the glasses as well as the pendants, and it is faster than asking.

Be careful with the geography here, because this industry is the most scattered in the atlas and the delta does not hold a monopoly on any of it. Rokid is in Hangzhou, XREAL in Beijing, Goertek, the largest AR and VR contract manufacturer, is in Shandong, and INMO designed its early glasses in TCL Technology Park in Nanshan before moving its headquarters to Chengdu in 2024. What remains concentrated here is worth the trip anyway: Shenzhen for the brands and for the waveguide optics that decide whether a display in a lens is even possible, and Dongguan for assembly at a scale nobody else offers. Read the delta's role in this category as supplier and builder to a global industry rather than as its author.

The export gate is the sharpest in this guide, and it is not a certification. CE, RED and FCC for the radio are routine. Consent is not. A device that records people who never agreed to be recorded is exposed in a way nothing else in this atlas is: two-party consent states in the United States make silently recording a private conversation a criminal matter, and in the EU the bystander's voice and face are personal data under GDPR with no household exemption once the wearer leaves their own home. The AI Act stacks further obligations on top wherever the system infers biometrics or emotion. Even Realities routes its European operation through a German entity, which tells you how seriously the category treats this. Treat a visible recording indicator, on-device deletion, a documented retention period and a straight answer to where the audio is processed as commercial requirements. Products in this category fail on the law, not the lab.

02 / Solution houses

The solution houses

01

RayNeo 雷鸟创新

Shenzhen

A member of the TCL family and the closest thing to an apex brand designing in the delta. Counterpoint Research put RayNeo first in the global AR glasses market in the third quarter of 2025 with 24 percent share. The range splits cleanly: the X series are AI and AR glasses with a display in the lens, headed by the X3 Pro, the Air series are portable viewers, with the Air 4 Pro claiming the first HDR10 display in AR glasses, and the V3 Slim is the camera-forward pair aimed squarely at what Meta made popular. Behind it sits TCL's display technology and panel supply chain, which is not an advantage a startup can buy.

02

Even Realities Displays

Shenzhen

Founded 2023 by ex-Apple engineers, with a chief executive who worked on the Apple Watch and the iPhone, and funded to a one billion dollar valuation in July 2026 on a 150 million dollar pre-Series B led by Meituan and Tencent. Worth studying because it is the deliberate counter-thesis to this whole page: the G1 and G2 put a display first and a camera nowhere, on the argument that people will wear glasses that show them things long before they accept glasses that film everyone else. It runs its European operation through a German entity for GDPR purposes. Whether or not the thesis holds, it is the most considered position on the consent problem in the category.

03

Lochn Optics 珑璟光电

Shenzhen

Founded 2014, and the reason a display can sit inside a lens at all. Lochn makes array and diffractive optical waveguide modules, the single hardest component in a pair of display glasses and the one that sets weight, brightness, field of view and price. Its base runs to 16,400 square metres of ultra-precision glass cold processing, optical coating and nanoimprint lines. It was the first company in China to mass-produce waveguide modules and is the only Chinese company inside the global top ten for AR waveguide patents; its one-dimensional reflective module shipped in the INMO Air 2. Camera glasses can skip this layer. Anything with a display in front of the eye cannot.

  • 2014founded
  • Shenzhenbase
  • 16,400 m²plant

04

Luxshare Precision 立讯精密

Dongguan · Qingxi

Founded May 2004 on Beihuan Road in Qingxi town, listed in Shenzhen as 002475 and chaired by Wang Laichun, Luxshare is the build layer at a scale that is difficult to picture: 416,197 employees and more than 110 production bases, the bulk of them in Guangdong. It runs the full span from connectors and modules up to complete system assembly for consumer electronics, which is precisely the service an American wearable brand needs when it is fifteen people and a Shopify store. This is not a house you brief with a product idea. It is a manufacturing partner you qualify, and the reason the pendant category can exist at all.
