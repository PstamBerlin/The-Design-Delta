01 / The landscape

The landscape

A video doorbell or security camera is three decisions wearing one shell. The first is the SoC: an Ingenic T-series, SigmaStar, Anyka or Fullhan video chip that encodes the stream and, increasingly, runs person-and-package detection on the device. (Sanctions pushed the category off HiSilicon years ago; the replacements are now the reference designs.) The second is the sensor, a SmartSens, GalaxyCore or Sony part that decides night vision and dynamic range. The third, and the one buyers underrate, is the cloud stack: the app, the servers, the notification pipeline. A battery doorbell adds a PIR-wake companion MCU so the main chip sleeps between visitors; that power design is where a good house earns its keep.

The category splits by mounting, not by chip. The wired video door phone (intercom, apartment systems, the installer trade), the battery WiFi doorbell in the Ring mould, the outdoor camera (bullet, PTZ, now solar-and-4G for anywhere without wiring), and the indoor pan-tilt. The live frontiers are on-device AI (person, pet, vehicle, package), AOV always-on-video at battery power budgets, and local storage sold as a feature rather than an apology, because the cloud is precisely what European buyers have learned to distrust.

The reference-design tell here is the app. Hundreds of Amazon brands ship the same camera running the same white-label cloud, one app under a thousand names, and when researchers pulled the thread on one such Shenzhen platform the finding was blunt: millions of streams, one company nobody had heard of, no GDPR compliance in sight. That is the story a buyer must engineer against, and it is why the serious houses now sell Tuya integration, EU data residency or a fully local mode as first-class features.

For export the gates stack higher than anywhere else in this atlas: CE and RED for the radio, RoHS, then GDPR reaching into firmware and cloud choices, the EU Cyber Resilience Act arriving on top, and for the US the NDAA question that took Hikvision and Dahua (both Hangzhou) off federal shopping lists. The apex splits between the West (Ring, Nest) and China’s own brands (eufy by Anker, TP-Link Tapo, Aqara, Reolink, EZVIZ), with the biggest hireable ODM, Meari, in Hangzhou outside this delta. The houses below are the PRD’s answer.

02 / Solution houses

The solution houses

01

Foscam Intelligent Technology

Shenzhen

Shenzhen Foscam Intelligent Technology (founded 2007) is the heritage house of the consumer IP camera, selling network cameras to the world before the doorbell wave had a name. Distribution in 80-plus countries, millions of units through retail, and a 300-plus network of distributors and OEM partners behind the brand, across indoor, outdoor, doorbell and NVR lines. Brand and contract both, the house that has survived every generation of this category, its security-scare years included, and kept shipping.

Longse Technology

Guangzhou · Huangpu

Longse (Guangzhou, founded 2006) is the white-label heavyweight and the page’s Guangzhou entry: CCTV cameras, NVRs and now WiFi, solar and 4G consumer lines, sold through 2,000-plus business partners in 150-plus countries. More than 1,000 staff, a cadence of new camera models every month, and CE, FCC and RoHS across the range. Where a distributor or brand goes for a full security catalogue under its own badge rather than a single hero product.

One of the larger dedicated surveillance factories in the consumer tier, the house for an own-brand camera range without building the firmware and cloud from scratch.

Eeguard Technology

Shenzhen · Bao’an (Fuhai)

Shenzhen Eeguard (founded 2012, Fuhai, Bao’an) is the video door phone specialist: wired intercom and apartment systems, 2-wire and IP variants, and the doorbell end of the trade, built by a 30-plus engineering team with its own SMT line down to the PCB. Already moving around 10,000 sets a month into the EU, with CE (EMC, LVD), FCC, REACH and RoHS on file. The installer-channel house of the set, where the doorbell is a building system rather than a gadget.
